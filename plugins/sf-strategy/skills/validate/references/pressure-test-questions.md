# Pressure-Test Questions

Six questions that expose fatal flaws before you spend money. Answer them honestly, not optimistically.

Ask only the questions mapped to the founder's stage. Three good answers beat six padded ones.

## Stage mapping

| Founder's stage | Ask |
|---|---|
| Pre-product — just an idea | Q1, Q2, Q3 |
| Prototype or early users | Q2, Q4, Q5 |
| Paying customers already | Q4, Q5, Q6 |

---

## Q1 — Demand reality

**What evidence do you have — beyond your own experience — that someone else actually wants this?**

Not "I think people need it." What have you seen, heard, or measured?

Scoring the answer:
- **Strong** — named people who have spent money or time on the problem, or a measured signal (search volume, forum threads, competitor reviews).
- **Weak** — "everyone I've talked to says it's a great idea."
- **None** — only the founder's own experience.

The founder's own pain is a legitimate starting signal but is never sufficient on its own. It is the single most common false positive in validation.

## Q2 — Status quo

**What are people in your field doing right now to handle this — even badly? What does that workaround cost them in time, money, or errors?**

If the founder can't describe the workaround in specific terms, they haven't watched anyone do the job.

> **The status quo is your real competitor** — not the other startup. It's the spreadsheet-and-email workaround people already live with. You have to be dramatically better than "good enough," and "good enough" is a very low bar that people are already used to.

## Q3 — Desperate specificity

**Name one specific person who needs this most.**

Not "dentists" — which dentist, at which practice, with what problem? If they can't name someone, they haven't found their customer yet.

> **"Everyone in my field needs this"** means you haven't found anyone specific yet. The more universal the need appears, the less validated it actually is.

## Q4 — Narrowest wedge

**What's the smallest version of this someone would pay for this week — not after you build the platform?**

One screen, one workflow, one outcome. If the answer takes more than two sentences, the wedge isn't narrow enough.

## Q5 — Observation

**Have you watched a colleague struggle with this task without helping them? What surprised you about how they actually do it vs. how you assumed?**

"Nothing surprised me" almost always means they haven't watched. Real observation always produces a surprise.

## Q6 — Future-fit

**How does your industry change in 3 years, and does that make this tool more essential or less?**

Guards against building for a workflow that is already being automated away by something bigger.

---

## The demand bar

> **Interest is not demand.** Waitlist signups are not demand. Demand is: someone would be genuinely upset if it disappeared.

Apply this to every answer. Enthusiasm, compliments, and "I'd definitely use that" are all interest. Money, time, and switching effort are demand.
